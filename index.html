Bringing pharmacogenomics to the molecular tumour board

Molecular tumour boards recommend targeted therapies based on the molecular profile of a tumour. PGxMTB is the first EU project to systematically integrate pharmacogenomics into these decisions, so that inherited and tumour-specific variation in drug metabolism and drug targets is taken into account.

How well a patient tolerates and responds to a cancer drug depends on genes that govern drug absorption, distribution, metabolism and excretion (ADME) and on drug targets – the field of pharmacogenomics (PGx). This information is rarely considered in molecular tumour board decisions today.

Building on germline and tumour multiomics data from the precision oncology programmes DKFZ/NCT/DKTK MASTER and MAPPYACTS1/2, the consortium develops a PGx pipeline covering more than 300 pharmacogenes, validates it in adult and paediatric cohorts, explores liver-derived extracellular vesicles as a liquid biopsy of hepatic drug metabolism, and prepares clinical implementation together with patient representatives.

What we aim to achieve

Predict PGx phenotypes

Combine germline and somatic variants with tumour multiomics data to predict drug response and toxicity, including the impact of rare variants.

Validate in independent cohorts

Confirm functional variants experimentally and validate PGx and drug-target signatures in adult and paediatric cancer patients.

Measure liver phenotypes

Use liver-derived extracellular vesicles as a minimally invasive liquid biopsy to capture hepatic drug metabolism in patients with cancer.

Implement in clinical practice

Establish a framework for PGx reporting in molecular tumour boards in Heidelberg and Paris, and train healthcare providers.

Involve patients

Engage patient representatives as research partners and develop materials that help patients understand PGx.

Evaluate the value

Assess the health-economic impact of implementing PGx in molecular tumour boards across European health systems.

Work packages

WP1

PGx pipeline & multiomics in MASTER

Refinement of a PGx pipeline for prediction of PGx phenotypes and drug targets in cancer patients, complemented by multiomics data in MASTER.

Tasks
  • T1.1 Germline PGx profiles including rare PGx variants for the selected PGxMTB drugs
  • T1.2 Somatic PGx profiles including rare PGx variants for the selected PGxMTB drugs
  • T1.3 PGx landscaping in multiomics data and identification of novel PGx signatures related to cancer drugs
Lead: IKP Stuttgart
WP2

Functional impact of rare PGx variation

Assessment of the functional impact of rare pharmacogenetic variation.

Tasks
  • T2.1 Systematic in silico functional predictions of rare variants in pharmacogenes
  • T2.2 In vitro experimental validation of candidate functional variants underlying ADME phenotypes
Lead: Karolinska Institutet
WP3

Validation & paediatric extension

Validation of multiomics data of pharmacogenes from MASTER in RATIONALE and extension to a paediatric and adolescent population from MAPPYACTS1/2.

Tasks
  • T3.1 Validation of molecular PGx and drug-target signatures in independent adult cohorts (MASTER2, RATIONALE)
  • T3.2 Extension to a paediatric and adolescent population from MAPPYACTS1/2
Lead: Gustave Roussy
WP4

Hepatic PGx phenotypes via liver EVs

Elucidation of hepatic PGx phenotypes of cancer patients using liver-derived extracellular vesicles to assess the impact of tumours.

Tasks
  • T4.1 Isolation of liver EVs and characterisation of EV isolates
  • T4.2 PGx phenotyping of liver EVs using proteomics, metabolomics and epigenetics
Lead: IKP Stuttgart
WP5

PGx implementation in MTBs & patient participation

Development of an implementation approach for PGx information in MTBs and involvement of patient participation.

Tasks
  • T5.1 Framework for PGx implementation in MTBs at NCT Heidelberg (MASTER) and Gustave Roussy (MAPPYACTS2)
  • T5.2 Enhancing patient engagement and training of healthcare providers for PGx
Lead: DKFZ / NCT Heidelberg
WP6

Health economic evaluation

Health economic (HE) evaluation of the PGx implementation in molecular tumour boards.

Lead: UMIT TIROL

Informationen für Patientinnen, Patienten und Angehörige

What is pharmacogenomics?

Our genes influence how the body processes medicines. Some people break down a drug faster, others more slowly. This can change how well a medicine works and how many side effects it causes. Pharmacogenomics (PGx) studies these inherited differences.

What does PGxMTB do?

In a molecular tumour board, cancer specialists discuss the genetic changes of a tumour and recommend a suitable treatment. PGxMTB investigates how information on a patient's own drug-processing genes can be added to these recommendations, so that cancer treatment becomes more effective and better tolerated.

Patients as research partners

The German Sarcoma Foundation, a patient organisation, is a partner in the consortium. Patient representatives help shape the research, review information materials and make sure that results are explained in an understandable way.

What does this mean for me?

PGxMTB is a research project. Its results are intended to improve cancer care in the future. If you have questions about your own treatment, please talk to your treating physician. This website cannot give individual medical advice.

Gene
A section of our DNA that contains the instructions for a protein, for example an enzyme that breaks down a medicine.
Germline
The genetic information we inherit and carry in every cell of the body, as opposed to changes that occur only in the tumour.
Molecular tumour board
A meeting of experts from different disciplines who use genetic test results to recommend a cancer treatment.

Was ist Pharmakogenomik?

Unsere Gene beeinflussen, wie der Körper Medikamente verarbeitet. Manche Menschen bauen einen Wirkstoff schneller ab, andere langsamer. Das kann verändern, wie gut ein Medikament wirkt und wie viele Nebenwirkungen es verursacht. Die Pharmakogenomik (PGx) untersucht diese angeborenen Unterschiede.

Was macht PGxMTB?

In einem molekularen Tumorboard besprechen Fachleute die genetischen Veränderungen eines Tumors und empfehlen eine passende Behandlung. PGxMTB untersucht, wie Informationen über die eigenen Gene, die Medikamente verarbeiten, in diese Empfehlungen einfließen können. Ziel ist eine Krebstherapie, die besser wirkt und besser vertragen wird.

Patientinnen und Patienten als Forschungspartner

Die Deutsche Sarkom-Stiftung, eine Patientenorganisation, ist Partnerin im Konsortium. Patientenvertreterinnen und -vertreter gestalten die Forschung mit, prüfen Informationsmaterialien und achten darauf, dass Ergebnisse verständlich erklärt werden.

Was bedeutet das für mich?

PGxMTB ist ein Forschungsprojekt. Die Ergebnisse sollen die Krebsbehandlung in Zukunft verbessern. Bei Fragen zu Ihrer eigenen Behandlung wenden Sie sich bitte an Ihre behandelnde Ärztin oder Ihren behandelnden Arzt. Diese Website kann keine individuelle medizinische Beratung geben.

Keimbahn

Molekulares Tumorboard
Eine Besprechung von Fachleuten verschiedener Disziplinen, die anhand genetischer Testergebnisse eine Krebsbehandlung empfehlen.
